The main idea

Direct payments always have two participants in the reported full scan. They also have no more than one currency conversion.

Participants are the banks involved in a payment path. An FX conversion happens when the payment changes currency.
Direct Bilateral Routing Accounts for 63.2% of Traffic vs. 32.6% Indirect & 4.2% Covered

This shows how many records use each route type. Direct is the largest group, followed by Indirect and Covered.

Direct Routes Cap FX at 1 Step, While Indirect & Covered Peak Sharply at 2 FX Steps

This compares the number of currency changes for each route type. In the reported full scan, Direct records have at most one conversion, while Indirect and Covered commonly have two.

What we found

Verified generator relationship: Every Direct transaction has exactly 2 participants and 0 or 1 FX conversions in the reported full scan. Indirect and Covered transactions have more varied participant and FX counts; their most common FX count is 2.

In simple words

Possible interpretation: Direct routes resemble bilateral settlement, while Indirect and Covered routes involve more participants and often more currency conversions. The synthetic fields do not independently establish the underlying account arrangements.

What to keep in mind

Continuity Coupling: Discontinuous paths (CONTINUITY_QFLAG='N') occur at 12.3% in Indirect/Covered vs. only 5.0% in Direct.

The main idea

A little over half of the payments take less than two minutes. Payments involving more participants tend to have longer recorded durations.

Duration is the recorded time for a payment. The relationship shown is an association; it does not prove that adding a participant causes a fixed delay.
Two common processing-time groups appear in the data

This groups records by how long they took to process. The two clusters show that processing times are not spread evenly across one smooth range.

Average duration rises by roughly 200 seconds across successive participant-count groups

This compares average duration across participant-count groups. The upward pattern is an association in the data; it does not by itself prove that adding a participant causes a delay.

What we found

Observed association: Mean TOTAL_DURATION increases across participant-count groups in the reported results: 663s (2 participants), 841s (3), 1,042s (4), 1,242s (5), 1,437s (6), and 1,649s (7). This is an association, not proof that each additional participant causes a fixed delay.

In simple words

Possible interpretation: Additional participants may correspond to more processing stages and longer elapsed times. The dataset does not identify the operational reason for each delay.

What to keep in mind

Rejected Hypothesis: 'More FX conversions cause longer settlement duration.' Disproved: Within 4-hop payments, median duration is 429s (0 FX), 418s (1 FX), 418s (2 FX), 418s (3 FX).

The main idea

The payment-detail fields contain structured tags that represent information such as names, addresses, and account details.

A token is a placeholder or tag that represents a piece of information. This page uses a 200,000-record sample for token analysis.
Top 10 Semantic Token Tags Extracted from Party Details (200,000-Row Sample)

This shows the most frequent structured tags found in party-detail text. The tags are placeholders for types of information, rather than the original personal details.

Dataset Structural Integrity & Graph Invariants Across All 10,000,000 Records

This summarises consistency checks across the full dataset. These checks help describe the structure and relationships in the generated records.

What we found

Observed structure: The 200,000-row reservoir sample contains 19 token-tag families, including character masks, street, city, IBAN, business, and name tags. The source CSV also contains embedded newlines in quoted detail fields, so physical line count is not the same as logical record count.

In simple words

Possible interpretation: Token fields appear designed to preserve structural clues about party details while masking personal information. The dataset does not establish which screening systems consume these fields.

What to keep in mind

Engineering Warning: Naive line-splitting tools (awk/wc) break on embedded newlines inside quoted *_DETAILS fields; always use an RFC-4180 CSV parser.
